## 1. Purpose

Cumberland Trust Federal Credit Union (CTFCU) is entrusted by 85,000 members with their financial information and assets. This Information Security Policy establishes management's commitment to protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of CTFCU information assets, and serves as the foundational document of the Information Security Management System (ISMS).

This policy satisfies the requirements of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 5.2 (Information Security Policy), the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314), and the cybersecurity expectations of the National Credit Union Administration (NCUA).

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All CTFCU employees, officers, directors, contractors, interns, and volunteers
- All information assets owned, leased, processed, transmitted, or stored by CTFCU, regardless of format (electronic, paper, verbal)
- All locations including the Knoxville headquarters, the Nashville disaster recovery site, all 15 branches, and all remote-work locations
- All third parties with access to CTFCU information assets, governed in conjunction with the Third-Party Risk Management Policy

## 3. Policy Statements

CTFCU is committed to:

**3.1** Protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of member nonpublic personal information (NPI), employee information, and proprietary business information against accidental or intentional disclosure, modification, or loss.

**3.2** Complying with all applicable laws, regulations, and contractual obligations relevant to information security, including the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, the Bank Secrecy Act, applicable state data protection laws, and NCUA cybersecurity guidance.

**3.3** Establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Information Security Management System aligned to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0.

**3.4** Identifying, assessing, and treating information security risks in accordance with the Risk Management Methodology, with formal residual risk acceptance decisions made by the appropriate management level based on risk severity.

**3.5** Providing the resources necessary to operate the ISMS, including budget, personnel, training, and tooling, as approved annually by the Board.

**3.6** Ensuring that all personnel understand their information security responsibilities through onboarding, annual training, and role-specific awareness programs.

**3.7** Holding all personnel accountable for compliance with this policy and supporting policies, standards, and procedures. Violations may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment and referral for criminal prosecution where applicable.

## 4. Information Security Objectives

The following objectives govern the ISMS for the period covered by this policy. Performance against these objectives is reviewed quarterly by the Information Security Steering Committee and reported annually to the Board.

## 5. Governance and Roles

**5.1 Board of Directors** holds ultimate accountability for the protection of CTFCU information assets and reviews the ISMS performance annually.

**5.2 Risk Committee** of the Board approves this policy, the Information Security Risk Appetite Statement, and material residual risk acceptance decisions above the high-risk threshold.

**5.3 Chief Executive Officer** sponsors the ISMS and ensures that information security is integrated with business strategy.

**5.4 Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)** is the Policy Owner. The CISO is responsible for the design, implementation, operation, and continual improvement of the ISMS; for reporting to the Risk Committee; and for serving as Qualified Individual under the GLBA Safeguards Rule.

**5.5 Information Security Steering Committee**, chaired by the CISO and including the CIO, COO, CFO, CHRO, Compliance Officer, and Physical Security Manager, meets quarterly to review ISMS performance, risk trends, incident summaries, and policy exceptions.

**5.6 Behavioral Threat Assessment Team (BTAT)**, chaired by the Chief Human Resources Officer with cross-functional membership, addresses behavioral and insider risks at the intersection of cyber, physical, and human factors.

**5.7 All employees and contractors** are responsible for protecting information assets in their custody, following established policies and procedures, completing required training, and reporting suspected information security incidents promptly.

## 6. Policy Framework

This policy is the foundation of the CTFCU policy library. Subordinate policies addressing specific control domains include — but are not limited to — Access Control, Acceptable Use, Third-Party Risk Management, Incident Response, and Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery. All subordinate policies must be consistent with this Information Security Policy.

Standards, procedures, and work instructions operationalize policy requirements and are maintained by the CISO in coordination with affected business owners.

## 7. Risk Management

CTFCU operates a formal risk management process aligned to ISO/IEC 27005 and NIST SP 800-30, documented in the Risk Management Methodology. The Risk Register is the authoritative record of all identified risks. All risks above the Risk Appetite Statement threshold require a documented treatment decision: mitigate, transfer, avoid, or accept.

Residual risk acceptance authority is tiered:

- **Critical residual risk** (score 20–25): Board Risk Committee approval required
- **Very High residual risk** (15–19): CEO approval required
- **High residual risk** (10–14): CISO approval required, with quarterly Steering Committee notification
- **Medium and below**: Risk Owner approval, recorded in the Risk Register

## 8. Convergence of Cyber, Physical, and Behavioral Risk

CTFCU explicitly recognizes that information security risk does not respect organizational silos. Threats originating in the physical environment (branch robbery, ATM tampering, workplace violence, social engineering at reception) and in the behavioral domain (insider misuse, domestic violence affecting employees, indicators of grievance or violence) directly affect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information assets.

Accordingly, the ISMS scope includes physical security controls, the Workplace Violence Prevention Program, and the Behavioral Threat Assessment Team. These programs operate as integrated components of information security governance rather than as separate functions.

## 9. Compliance and Exceptions

Compliance with this policy is monitored through the internal audit program, management reviews, control self-assessments, and continuous monitoring by the CISO function. Exceptions require documented justification, compensating controls, expiration date, and CISO approval. Exceptions are recorded in the ISMS Exception Register and reviewed quarterly.

## 10. Review and Improvement

This policy is reviewed at least annually by the CISO and approved by the Risk Committee. Reviews are also triggered by:

- Material changes to CTFCU's operating environment, technology, or third-party relationships
- Significant information security incidents
- Changes in applicable laws, regulations, or industry standards
- Internal or external audit findings indicating policy inadequacy

Continuous improvement is operationalized through the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle defined in ISO 27001 Clause 10.
